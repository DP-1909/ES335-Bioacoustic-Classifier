Section 1 • Problem Formulation & Acoustic Data Audit

Research Question, "One Row of Data" & ESC-50 Cohort

Passive acoustic monitoring enables continuous, non-invasive biodiversity tracking across remote habitats without human field observers, but requires compact classifiers that distinguish overlapping biological vocalizations from ambient weather textures.

1. Core Research Question

Can compact, time-averaged spectral and cepstral descriptors ($17$ handcrafted features) reliably classify $19$ wildlife and natural soundscape categories without deep neural networks, and how strongly does decision-boundary geometry (linear vs. kernel vs. tree ensemble) govern multi-class separability?

2. What One Row Represents

Unit of Analysis ($N = 760$): One row represents a single $5\text{-second}$ mono WAV recording sampled at $44.1\text{ kHz}$ ($220,500$ raw audio samples), compressed into a $17\text{-dimensional}$ continuous numerical feature vector $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{17}$ paired with a discrete target label $y_i \in \{1, \dots, 19\}$.

3. Prediction-Time & Non-Causal Scope

Prediction-Time Setup: An edge acoustic sensor buffers a $5\text{s}$ window, extracts the $17$ features on-device, and outputs class probabilities—with zero metadata or future labels available. Predictions reflect statistical similarity in time-averaged frequency envelopes rather than causal ecological claims.

Dataset Provenance & Quality Audit

  • Source & Access: Curated from Karol Piczak's public CC-licensed ESC-50 Dataset (2,000 recordings across 50 environmental classes).
  • Balanced Subset ($N = 760$): All $19$ wildlife and natural environment classes were extracted ($40\text{ clips/class}$; random-guess baseline $= 1/19 = 5.26\%$).
  • Predefined Fold Integrity: Each class has exactly $8\text{ clips}$ in each of the $5$ official ESC-50 folds ($152\text{ clips/fold}$), ensuring takes from the same original Freesound recording never split across folds.
  • Data Quality Verification: Automated audit confirmed 0 missing values, 0 corrupted WAV headers, and 0 duplicate clip IDs. Real-world acoustic challenges include background wind/insect cross-talk and variable recording gain across Freesound contributors.

Section 2 • Preprocessing Choices & Feature Engineering

Acoustic Signal Representation & Leak-Free Standardization

Raw $5\text{s}$ waveforms ($220,500$ dimensions) suffer from severe curse-of-dimensionality on $N = 760$ samples. We project each clip onto $17$ domain-motivated spectral, cepstral, and time-domain descriptors via librosa.

Feature Group Variables Dims Acoustic & Physical Rationale
Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients MFCC 1 – MFCC 13 (frame mean) 13 Models the log-power spectrum warped onto the perceptual Mel scale, capturing vocal-tract resonance and timbral envelope (e.g., cow vs. crow).
Spectral Centroid Spectral Centroid (Hz) 1 Center of mass of the magnitude spectrum; separates high-frequency stridulation (crickets, chirping_birds) from low-frequency rumbles (thunderstorm).
Spectral Bandwidth Spectral Bandwidth (Hz) 1 Second central moment around the centroid; distinguishes narrowband harmonic calls from broadband noise (rain, wind, sea_waves).
Zero-Crossing Rate Zero-Crossing Rate (rate) 1 Waveform sign-change frequency; spikes during unvoiced sibilants, insect stridulation, and percussive crackles (crackling_fire).
RMS Frame Energy RMS Energy (amplitude) 1 Root-mean-square acoustic intensity distinguishing sparse transient events (water_drops) from sustained high-energy soundscapes (pouring_water).
Zero-Leakage Scaling Protocol: Because Spectral Centroid ($\sim 10^3\text{ Hz}$) and Zero-Crossing Rate ($\sim 10^{-2}$) span five orders of magnitude, Euclidean distances in RBF-SVM and $L_2$ penalties in Logistic Regression would be dominated by Hz-scale features. We apply $z = (x - \mu_{\text{train}}) / \sigma_{\text{train}}$ inside sklearn.pipeline.Pipeline so $\mu_{\text{train}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{train}}$ are estimated strictly on training folds and never touch validation or outer test folds.

Section 3 • Baseline, Methods & Leak-Free Evaluation

Model Paradigms & 5-Fold Nested Cross-Validation

We contrast a linear baseline against two non-linear paradigms under strict 5-fold nested cross-validation: each outer fold holds out $1$ complete ESC-50 predefined fold ($152\text{ clips}$) while an inner $4\text{-fold}$ GridSearchCV optimizes Macro-F1 on the remaining $4\text{ folds}$ ($608\text{ clips}$).

Baseline: Logistic Regression ($L_2$)

Core Idea: Multinomial linear hyperplanes $\mathbf{w}_k^\top \mathbf{z} + b_k$ regularized by $L_2$ penalty ($C \in \{0.1, 1, 10\}$).
Appropriateness & Limit: Fast, convex, interpretable baseline; assumes classes are linearly separable in $17\text{-D}$ feature space ($50.69\%$ Macro-F1 ceiling).

Candidate 1: Random Forest (200 Trees)

Core Idea: Bootstrap ensemble of $200$ decorrelated CART decision trees partitioning feature space via axis-aligned thresholds (min_samples_split=5).
Appropriateness & Limit: Captures non-linear feature interactions without scaling sensitivity, though orthogonal splits struggle with diagonal spectral correlations ($50.74\%$ Macro-F1).

Champion: RBF-Kernel SVM

Core Idea: Maximum-margin One-vs-One classifier mapping standardized features into an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space via $K(\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{z}') = \exp(-\gamma \|\mathbf{z} - \mathbf{z}'\|^2)$ ($C=1, \gamma=\text{scale}$).
Why It Wins: Smooth radial boundaries isolate curved acoustic manifolds ($56.68\%$ Macro-F1).

Section 4 • Quantitative Results & Error Analysis

$19 \times 19$ Confusion Matrix & Acoustic Failure Modes

Inspect all $760$ out-of-fold test predictions across SVM (RBF), Random Forest, and Logistic Regression. Diagonal cells show per-class recall out of $40$ recordings.

Error Mode 1: Broadband Water & Wind Overlap

Stationary broadband textures—specifically rain, pouring_water, sea_waves, and wind—exhibit nearly identical time-averaged spectral centroids and bandwidths, generating the largest cluster of off-diagonal confusions.

Error Mode 2: High-Frequency Stridulation

Crickets, insects, and chirping_birds concentrate energy above $3.5\text{ kHz}$ with high Zero-Crossing Rates; field recordings with background insect chorus occasionally shift bird clips into insect classes.

Error Mode 3: Transient vs. Continuous Dynamics

Discrete impulsive sounds (water_drops, crackling_fire) lose their temporal onset spikes under $5\text{s}$ mean pooling, causing occasional confusion with continuous background textures.

Section 6 • Scientific Limitations, Reproducibility & Citations

Limitations, Reproduction Protocol & Responsible Sharing

Scientific & Modeling Limitations

  • Temporal Pooling Bottleneck: Averaging frame-level features across all $5\text{s}$ discards transient onset bursts (e.g., individual water_drops vs. steady rain). Adding frame-level standard deviation, $\Delta\text{-MFCCs}$, and spectral contrast is the primary next step.
  • Closed-Set Assumption: Classifiers assume every test clip belongs strictly to one of the $19$ target classes and lack an out-of-distribution rejection threshold for anthropogenic noise (sirens, engines).
  • Sample Size ($N = 760$): With $32\text{ training clips/class}$ per outer fold, high-capacity models risk variance on rare acoustic variants; deeper 2D Log-Mel CNNs (PANNs / YAMNet) require transfer learning rather than training from scratch.

Step-by-Step Reproducibility Guide

  • 1. Interactive Colab Notebook: Open our Google Colab Notebook (View Mode) or clone https://github.com/DP-1909/ES335-Bioacoustic-Classifier.git.
  • 2. Dataset & Split Setup: Download ESC-50 (meta/esc50.csv + audio/), filter the $19$ target categories ($760\text{ clips}$), and preserve the predefined fold column ($1\text{–}5$) for leak-free nested CV.
  • 3. Run Pipeline: Execute the notebook (Python 3.10+, librosa, scikit-learn, numpy, pandas) to extract the $17$ features, run 5-fold nested GridSearchCV, and export site_data.json & models_export.json.
  • 4. Local Server: Run python -m http.server 8000 in the root directory and open http://localhost:8000.
