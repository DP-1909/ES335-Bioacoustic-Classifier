01 • Problem Statement & Motivation

Why Classical Acoustic Feature Engineering Matters for Biodiversity Monitoring

Ecological Motivation

Passive acoustic monitoring enables non-invasive tracking of wildlife populations (birds, amphibians, insects, mammals) and weather events (rain, wind, thunderstorms) across remote habitats without continuous human field presence.

Core Classification Task

Given a 5-second environmental audio clip x, predict its discrete acoustic source class y ∈ {1, &dots;, 19} using compact summary statistics extracted from time-frequency representations.

Why Compare Linear, Kernel & Tree Models?

Acoustic classes exhibit non-linear spectral overlaps (e.g., rain vs. pouring_water vs. sea_waves). Comparing Logistic Regression, RBF-SVM, and Random Forest reveals how decision-boundary geometry affects multi-class separability.

02 • Dataset & 17-Feature Pipeline

Curated ESC-50 Wildlife & Nature Subset

From the 2,000 recordings in Karol Piczak's ESC-50 dataset, we curated all 19 wildlife and natural environment classes (40 clips per class = 760 clips; 8 clips per class in each of the 5 predefined folds; zero missing or duplicate rows).

17 Extracted Acoustic Features (librosa)

  • Features 1–13 (MFCC 1–13): Mean Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients capturing vocal tract / timbral envelope across perceptual Mel bands.
  • Feature 14 (Spectral Centroid): Mean center-of-mass of the magnitude spectrum in Hz (perceptual brightness).
  • Feature 15 (Spectral Bandwidth): Mean spread of frequencies around the centroid in Hz (tonal vs. broadband noise).
  • Feature 16 (Zero-Crossing Rate): Mean sign-change rate of the time-domain waveform (percussive / high-frequency energy).
  • Feature 17 (RMS Energy): Mean root-mean-square frame amplitude capturing overall loudness dynamics.

04 • Methodology & Hyperparameter Tuning

Leak-Free 5-Fold Nested Cross-Validation

To prevent data leakage across related recordings, each outer iteration holds out 1 complete ESC-50 fold (152 clips) for testing while running inner 4-fold GridSearchCV (optimizing Macro-F1) on the remaining 4 folds (608 clips).

05 • Results & Error Analysis

19×19 Confusion Matrix & Acoustic Failure Modes

Switch between SVM (RBF), Random Forest, and Logistic Regression to inspect class-level recall (diagonal) and the most frequently confused sound pairs across all 760 outer-fold test predictions.

1. Broadband Water & Wind Overlap

Continuous broadband textures such as rain, pouring_water, sea_waves, and wind share stationary spectral envelopes, causing the largest share of off-diagonal confusions when temporal dynamics are averaged out.

2. High-Frequency Stridulation & Chirps

Crickets, insects, and chirping_birds concentrate energy above 3.5 kHz with elevated Zero-Crossing Rates, occasionally overlapping when background insect noise appears in bird recordings.

3. Why RBF-SVM Outperforms Linear Baseline

Mapping standardized 17-D features into a non-linear Gaussian kernel space improves Macro-F1 by +5.99 percentage points over Logistic Regression (56.68% vs. 50.69%) and +5.94 points over Random Forest.

06 • Limitations & Next Steps

Future Improvements

  • Temporal Pooling: Clip-level mean pooling discards transient envelope bursts (e.g., individual water_drops vs. steady rain). Adding standard deviation, delta-MFCCs, and spectral contrast will capture temporal modulation.
  • Log-Mel Spectrogram Representations: Pairing handcrafted features with 2D CNN embeddings (PANNs / YAMNet) can resolve broadband environmental textures.
  • Probability Calibration: Applying Platt scaling (probability=True) on SVC will provide calibrated confidence intervals for ecological deployment.
